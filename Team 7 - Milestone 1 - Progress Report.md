**Product or situation**: Viecnhanh is a platform that matches Vietnamese workers with suitable jobs in Aichi, Japan. The process is simple and short, and workers get results fast. A worker sends their profile through a messaging app on their phone and gets a reply within 48 hours. Today, workers in Japan mostly find jobs through friends and Facebook/LINE groups. ###  **Key Finding 1: Severe Blue-Collar Shortage in Aichi Drives High Dependency on Costly Dispatch Agencies (*Haken*)**

* **Brief / Client Quote:** *"The key client is opening a new factory in Aichi, a major manufacturing region in Japan facing severe labour shortages."* (Project Brief – CEO Ms. Ngoc Hoang)  
* **Statistical Data:** According to Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW), Japan reached approximately 2.3 to 2.6 million foreign workers by 2025–2026, with **Aichi Prefecture ranking 2nd nationwide (\~9.7% of Japan's total foreign workforce, or \~210,000–250,000+ workers)**. Manufacturing accounts for roughly **24.7% to 30%** of foreign employment in the region, and the effective job openings-to-applicants ratio for factory production roles in Aichi typically stays around **1.5x–2.0x** (roughly 2 openings for every 1 applicant).  
* **Operational Observation:** Because domestic Japanese youth generally avoid "3K" factory work (*Kitsui, Kitanai, Kiken* – Demanding, Dirty, Dangerous), newly opened factories in Aichi usually rely on staffing agencies (*Haken-gaisha*) or Registered Support Organizations (*Touroku Shien Kikan*), which typically charge a **\~25%–35% markup** on hourly wages. Key clients want a direct platform like ViệcNhanh to bypass these agency markups while filling 100–300+ ramp-up shift positions quickly.  
* **Traceable Sources:**  
  * MHLW Report on Foreign Worker Employment Status (*Gaikokujin Koyou Joukyou*): `[https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/newpage_37084.html](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/newpage_37084.html)`  
  * Aichi Labour Bureau (Aichi Roudoukyoku) Employment Statistics: `[https://jsite.mhlw.go.jp/aichi-roudoukyoku/](https://jsite.mhlw.go.jp/aichi-roudoukyoku/)`

**Key Finding 2: The "48-Hour Placement" Bottleneck Lies in Legal Visa Compliance, Not Just Candidate Matching**

* **Brief / Client Quote:** *"Bring the full model unchanged: sign-up by message, placement within 48 hours and daily wage advances, to serve the key client."* (CEO Ms. Ngoc Hoang)  
* pan's **Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act (Article 73-2)**, employers hiring foreign workers without valid work authorization face penalties of up to **3 million JPY in fines and/or up to 3 years of imprisonment** (*Fuhou Shuurou Jochouzai*). Additionally, cross-border visa processing (Certificate of Eligibility – COE) typically takes **1 to 3+ months**, while part-time work for Student/Family visa holders (*Shikakugai Katsudou Kyoka*) is strictly capped at **28 hours per week**.  
* **Operational Observation:** A factory in Aichi cannot legally onboard a worker from overseas within 48 hours. Achieving a 48-hour placement is generally only feasible whvalid Residence Cards (*Zairyu Card*). Currently, factory HR teams inspect *Zairyu Cards* and work-hour limits manually, creating a major compliance bottleneck that delays onboarding beyond 48 hours.  
* **Traceable Sources:**  
* en recruiting **foreign or domestic workers already residing in Japan** who hold Immigration Services Agency of Japan (ISA) – Rules for Employers of Foreign Nationals: `[https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/publications/materials/nyuukokukanri07_00058.html](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/publications/materials/nyuukokukanri07_00058.html)`  
  * e-Gov Japan Law Portal – Immigration Control Act (Article 73-2): `[https://elaws.e-gov.go.jp/](https://elaws.e-gov.go.jp/)`

### **Key Finding 3: Channel & Data Mismatch — Unstructured Zalo Sign-up Fails in Japan's Factory Ecosystem**

* **Brief / Client Quote:** *"Connects 1.5 million blue-collar workers with factories, warehouses and restaurants. Sign-up via Zalo..."* (Project Brief)  
* **Market Data:** According to Japanese telecommunications and social media usage reports (Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications – MIC), **LINE is used by approximately 90%–94% of Japan's population across all age groups**, whereas Zalo is primarily restricted to the Vietnamese community. Meanwhile, Aichi's foreign manufacturing workforce is multi-national, consisting primarily of **Vietnamese (\~25%–30%), Brazilian, Filipino, Indonesian, and Chinese workers**, managed by **Japanese HR and Floor Managers**.  
* **Operational Observation:** Keeping sign-up exclusively on Zalo isolates Japanese factory managers and non-Vietnamese workers in Aichi. Furthermore, free-text messaging sign-up lacks structured compliance fields required by Japanese factory HR before day one (specifically: *Zairyu Card* IC verification, *My Number* declaration, commuting route allowance, and Japanese bank/Yucho account details).  
* **Traceable Sources:**  
  * MIC Japan Information and Communications White Paper (Social Media Usage Rates): `[https://www.soumu.go.jp/johotsusintokei/whitepaper/](https://www.soumu.go.jp/johotsusintokei/whitepaper/)`  
  * Aichi Prefectural Government – Multicultural Coexistence & Foreign Resident Statistics: `[https://www.pref.aichi.jp/soshiki/tabunka/](https://www.pref.aichi.jp/soshiki/tabunka/)`

### **Key Finding 4: Mandatory Pre-Shift Safety Training (*Anzen Kyoiku*) & Language Barriers Drive Early Factory Turnover**

* **Interview / Observation Note:** *New foreign workers often panic or quit in the first week because they cannot read Kanji safety signs or understand the floor leader's instructions*  
* **Statutory / Industry Data:** Under **Article 59 of Japan's Industrial Safety and Health Act (*Roudou Anzen Eisei Hou*)**, factories are legally required to provide occupational safety and health education (*Anzen Eisei Kyoiku*) immediately upon hiring a new worker before assigning them to hazardous or mechanized production lines. In Japanese blue-collar manufacturing and logistics, early turnover within the first 30 days typically ranges from **\~15% to 25%**, largely due to workplace cultural shock (5S rules) and communication breakdown.  
* **Operational Observation:** Factory line managers in Aichi hesitate to accept "48-hour fast-placed workers" on the shop floor because untrained workers who cannot read Kanji machine warnings pose severe industrial accident risks, which can trigger mandatory line shutdowns by the Labour Standards Inspection Office (*Roudou Kijun Kantokusho*).  
* **Traceable Sources:**  
  * Japan Industrial Safety and Health Act (Article 59 – Safety Education at the Time of Employment): `[https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/](https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/)`  
  * MHLW Workplace Safety Portal (*Anzen Project*) – Multilingual Safety Materials for Foreign Workers: `[https://anzeninfo.mhlw.go.jp/](https://anzeninfo.mhlw.go.jp/)`

### **Key Finding 5: Wage Advance Fees Generate \~35% of Core Revenue, Requiring an "Earned Wage Access (EWA)" Structure to Avoid Japan's Money Lending Classification**

* **`Client Interview Quote (CEO Ms. Ngoc Hoang):`**  
  * *`English:`* `"Wage advance fees account for about 35% of our revenue, making it a major segment. The rest (~65%) mainly comes from placement fees paid by enterprises per referred worker... Honestly, I am somewhat worried about the wage advance segment. In some countries, it can be classified as money lending, so we need to examine it carefully. However, it is still a core part of the model I want to keep."`  
  * *`Original Vietnamese Quote:`* `"Phí ứng lương chiếm khoảng 35% doanh thu, nên nó là một mảng khá lớn. Phần còn lại chủ yếu là phí doanh nghiệp trả cho mỗi lao động mình giới thiệu... Thật ra mình cũng hơi lo về mảng ứng lương. Ở một số nước, nó có thể bị coi là hoạt động cho vay, nên cần xem kỹ. Nhưng đó vẫn là phần mình muốn giữ trong mô hình."`  
* **`Revenue & Statutory Data:`**  
  * **`Internal Revenue Split:`** `~35% from Wage Advance Fees (Phí ứng lương) + ~65% from B2B Per-Hire Placement Fees (Phí giới thiệu nhân sự thu từ doanh nghiệp).`  
  * **`Japanese Legal Framework:`** `Under Japan's Money Lending Business Act (Kashikingyouhou) and rulings by the Financial Services Agency (FSA – Kinyuuchou), if a third-party platform advances funds beyond confirmed worked hours or purchases a worker's wage claim (Kyuuyo Factoring), it is legally classified as Money Lending (Kashikingyou). Operating without a money-lending license violates the Act, and charging percentage-based fees that exceed 15%–20% annualized interest violates the Interest Rate Restriction Act (Risoku Seigen Hou). Furthermore, Article 24 of the Labor Standards Act mandates that wages be paid directly by the employer to the worker.`  
* **`Operational & Product Observation:`**  
  * `ViệcNhanh cannot drop the daily wage advance feature without losing ~35% of its revenue, nor can it operate as an unlicensed lender in Aichi.`  
  * `In Japan, compliant platforms (locally known as Kyuryo Maebarai SaaS, such as Payme or JobPay) avoid "money lending" classification by structuring the flow under Employer Welfare / Earned Wage Access (EWA):`  
    1. `Workers can only withdraw a portion (typically ~50%–70%) of wages already earned from verified, completed shifts (leaving the remaining ~30%–50% buffer for mandatory Japanese withholding tax Gensen Choushuu and social insurance).`  
    2. `Legally, the payment is an advance on earned labor compensation (Roudou no Taika) authorized by the Employer, not a loan (Kashitsuke).`  
    3. `Instead of charging loan interest (Risoku), the platform charges a transparent, flat System Usage / Real-time Transfer Administrative Fee (System Riyou Tesuuryou) per transaction (or bundles the fee with the factory's B2B service package).`  
* **`Traceable Sources:`**  
  * `Primary Source: Client Room Interview with CEO Ms. Ngoc Hoang (Team Interview Notes).`  
  * `Financial Services Agency of Japan (FSA) – Warning & Legal Classification on Wage Factoring / Unlicensed Lending (Kyuuyo Factoring): [https://www.fsa.go.jp/ordinary/chuui/kashikin.html](https://www.fsa.go.jp/ordinary/chuui/kashikin.html)`  
  * `Japan Labor Standards Act (Article 24 – Direct Payment of Wages & Article 25 – Emergency Wage Advance): [https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/koyou_roudou/roudoukijun/](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/koyou_roudou/roudoukijun/)`

## **5\. Opportunity \+ solutions** 

Which problem and solution direction did your team choose, and why? Link your choice back to the findings in section 3\.

> **Example**

> * Chosen problem: students cannot tell which study spaces are free before they walk there.  
> * Opportunity: showing live availability before students leave where they are could save walking time and make exam weeks less stressful.  
> * Solution direction: a map-based mobile app that shows free seats and rooms, with one-tap booking.  
> * Why this direction: it targets the frustration students mentioned most (31 of 42 survey respondents gave up looking at least once). We also considered longer library hours, but that is outside our control.

**Your answer**

Chosen problem: Skilled Vietnamese blue-collar workers already living in Aichi can’t quickly find a job that fits their skills and pays fairly. They rely on unverified Facebook/LINE posts or costly agencies. The few fast offers they do get often fall through because of visa checks, paperwork or safety-training requirements.

Opportunity: Viecnhanh’s promise is a placement within 48 hours. It’s realistic only for workers who are already in Japan with a valid Residence Card (Finding 2). This is exactly our target user. If their visa status, skills and documents are checked before a factory sees the profile, and the pay is shown as one clear take-home figure, workers can choose a job with confidence. Factories also get a direct hiring channel without paying a 25–35% agency markup (Finding 1).

Solution direction: A mobile-first, message-based job-matching service in Vietnamese, with five parts:

* Structured chat sign-up on the messaging apps workers already use (Zalo or LINE). A short guided flow collects Residence Card status, allowed working hours, skills and certifications (welding, forklift, machine operation), and bank details (Finding 3).  
* Visa pre-check so each worker sees only jobs they can legally take, and factory HR receives profiles that are already checked (Finding 2).  
* Clear pay cards in Vietnamese that turn each job post into one take-home figure, including shifts, overtime, conditional bonuses, dorm costs and deductions.  
* Pre-shift safety onboarding in Vietnamese (short videos and visual guides to Kanji safety signs) completed before day one. This meets Article 59 and reduces first-week dropouts (Finding 4).  
* Daily pay advance as Earned Wage Access: a partner fronts the wages already earned based on verified attendance, and the factory keeps its monthly payroll (Finding 5).

Why this direction: It keeps the client’s core model (sign-up by message, placement in 48 hours) and changes only what Japanese law and factory operations require (Findings 2, 4 and 5). It targets the pain points that block both sides: unclear pay and unverified channels for workers, and compliance and safety risk for factories.
